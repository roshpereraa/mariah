All I Want for Christmas is you

Mariah Carey

Four minutes of sleigh bells and a key change, written to sound like it came out in 1963 rather than 1994. It is playing now, and it will keep playing. Side B is five games.

Sleeve notes

Written on the back, as is traditional

Carey and Walter Afanasieff wrote it for Merry Christmas, her fourth album, out in October 1994. The brief was less a 1990s ballad than a Phil Spector record: sleigh bells from the first bar, piano hammering eighth notes, backing vocals stacked until they stop sounding like people.

It did not go to number one. Not in 1994, and not for a long while after. Christmas singles were kept off the Hot 100 by the chart rules of the day, and by the time those rules changed the song had quietly become something closer to infrastructure.

It finally reached the top in December 2019, twenty-five years late, as Carey's nineteenth number one. It has gone back every December since, which is less a comeback than a season.

What makes it work is the restraint in the lyric. No list, no North Pole, no snow. She turns down every Christmas thing on offer and asks for one that is not on the table. The arrangement spends four minutes disagreeing with her at full volume.

“Make my wish come true — all I want for Christmas is you.”

Side B

Five tracks

Games, not songs. Each runs in the page and remembers your best score on this device. The music keeps going underneath.

The sound

Streamed from the official upload through YouTube's embed player. If that is blocked, an original sleigh-bell arrangement is generated in your browser instead, so the room is never quiet.

The cursor

A reindeer. Its nose glows, it grins while you hold a click, and it leaves a short trail of dust behind it.

The build

Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no dependencies. The snow is a canvas; the fallback music is an oscillator.
